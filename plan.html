OUR HOME. YOUR FUTURE.

A practical plan for Grand Valley.

I believe good municipal leadership starts with fiscal accountability, listening to residents and making decisions based on what our community needs today and where we are going tomorrow.

My approach is not built around promises that property taxes will go down. It is built around making sure we are doing everything we reasonably can to manage municipal costs, deliver value and strengthen the revenue base that supports our community.

FISCAL ACCOUNTABILITY

Review Services. Find Efficiencies. Drive Business Growth.

01

Review Services

We should regularly review the services and programs the Town provides, whether they remain necessary, and whether the current service level makes sense for a community of our size.

02

Find Efficiencies

For the services we continue to provide, we should examine how they are delivered and look for cost savings, better processes and more efficient ways to achieve the same or better results.

03

Drive Business Growth

A broader commercial and industrial assessment base can generate additional municipal revenue and help reduce the amount of pressure placed on residential taxpayers.

Fiscal responsibility means understanding the numbers, questioning costs and making sure every dollar can be accounted for. That has been a consistent focus of mine on Grand Valley Council, and it would remain a priority as Mayor.

PLANNING FOR MORE GROWTH

Matching our decisions to the pace of growth.

Grand Valley is growing and will continue to grow. But growth does not always happen at the same pace.

The Town added services and made investments during a period when rapid development was expected to continue. Housing starts have slowed, and that means some of those decisions may have come ahead of when the community actually needed them.

We should evaluate where we are now, what residents need today and what services will be required as development picks up again. Planning ahead is important, but so is making sure the timing and cost of municipal decisions make sense.

We should also be proactive about attracting appropriate businesses and investment. More business growth means more local opportunity and a stronger tax base to help support municipal services.

LEADERSHIP MATTERS

How I believe a Mayor should lead.

Listens

A good Mayor listens to residents and makes sure their needs and concerns are considered when decisions are made.

Finds Compromise

A good Mayor works to find common ground and build support for practical solutions that move Grand Valley forward.

Is Decisive

A good Mayor is prepared to provide direction, make decisions and keep Council focused on the priorities residents expect us to deliver.

EXPERIENCE & ACCOUNTABILITY

Prepared to do the work.

As a business owner, I understand the importance of controlling costs and being accountable for results. As a sales executive, I have built a career around relationships, negotiation and finding practical solutions.

During more than five years on Grand Valley Council, one of my strengths has been focusing on the numbers and fiscal accountability — asking questions, understanding costs and making sure taxpayer dollars are accounted for.

I have also served as a Board Member with both AMO and ROMA, giving me experience advocating for municipal priorities and building relationships with municipal and provincial leaders.
