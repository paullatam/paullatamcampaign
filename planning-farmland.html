PLANNING & AGRICULTURE

Planning for Growth While Protecting Farmland

Grand Valley can continue to grow while respecting the agricultural land that has helped shape our community and remains vital to rural Ontario.

Grand Valley is growing, and we need to plan for that growth. But as we make decisions about where and how our community develops, we also need to recognize the value of the agricultural land around us.

During my time on Council, I have consistently advocated for planning decisions that respect and protect farmland. That included supporting tighter rules around surplus farm dwelling severances to help limit the unnecessary fragmentation of agricultural land.

I have also been involved as Grand Valley worked through changes to our Official Plan and Zoning By-law. Throughout those discussions, one of my priorities has been considering the long-term impact our decisions can have on agriculture.

Once housing and infrastructure are built on farmland, that land cannot be returned to agricultural production.

That matters because agriculture isn't simply part of Grand Valley's character. It is an important part of our local economy and a major economic driver throughout rural Ontario.

My involvement with ROMA also gave me the opportunity to broaden my understanding of the challenges facing rural municipalities across the province. Growth, infrastructure, agriculture and land-use planning are interconnected issues, and communities across Ontario are trying to find the right balance.

Protecting farmland doesn't mean opposing growth.

It means planning growth responsibly.

We should make efficient use of land already designated for development, carefully consider when additional land is needed, and understand the long-term consequences before productive agricultural land is converted to other uses.

Grand Valley will continue to change. Good planning means accommodating the homes, businesses and services we need while respecting the agricultural land that has helped shape our community.

We can grow Grand Valley while still protecting what makes rural Ontario worth calling home.

Our Home. Your Future.

Paul Latam
