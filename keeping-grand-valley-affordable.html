FROM PAUL

Keeping Grand Valley Affordable While Planning for the Future

Budget and taxes are the number one concern I hear when talking to residents. Here is how I would approach that challenge.

When I talk to residents in Grand Valley, one concern comes up again and again: the cost of living is going up, and property taxes are part of that pressure.

That is why I believe the municipal budget and taxes will be the number one issue in this election.

The tax increases we've had are not sustainable, and I would propose to keep them near the rate of inflation or less. Taxes will not go down, but if we can review what services we provide, we can try to keep them down.

This will take a team approach, both locally and at the County. It requires Council to have difficult conversations, look carefully at priorities and be willing to ask what we keep, cut or reduce when it comes to the services we provide.

Reviewing the Services We Provide

The first place we need to look is at the services the Town currently provides and to evalute the merits of them. In doing the evaluation, it does not mean cutting for the sake of cutting. It means asking practical questions and making calculated choices.

  • Do we still need the service at the level we currently provide it?
  • Are there services that could be reduced without negatively affecting residents?
  • Are there areas where the Town is providing more than is necessary for a municipality our size?

Reviewing areas such as parks and recreation, by-law services and other municipal operations. Every service has a cost, and residents deserve to know that the services they are paying for are being delivered at an appropriate level and providing real value. If there's opportunity for revenue growth from somehwere else other then the tax base, this should be evaluated as well.

Finding Better Ways to Deliver Services

The second part of the conversation is efficiency. Not every saving has to come from reducing a service. We also need to look at how services are delivered.

There may be opportunities to reduce costs through better purchasing, improved processes, different service delivery models or other efficiencies within the budget. Sometimes small changes across several departments can add up to meaningful savings.

The goal should be to maintain the services residents value while making sure we are delivering them as efficiently as possible.

Growing the Business Tax Base

The third piece is business growth. One of the best long-term ways to reduce pressure on residential taxpayers is to grow the commercial and industrial tax base.

Grand Valley has employment lands with real potential. The pumping station that will help service those lands is moving forward, with completion currently anticipated around late 2027 or early 2028.

But we should not wait until that infrastructure is complete before we start planning. We can make sure our planning framework is ready, determine how we want to market those lands and position Grand Valley as a community where businesses want to invest, create jobs and become part of the community.

If we are successful in attracting new businesses, we broaden the tax base and help reduce the amount of pressure placed on residential property owners.

Leadership Matters

These conversations will not always be easy. A good mayor needs to be able to listen, find compromise and make decisions.

LISTENS. Residents, Council and staff all need to know that their concerns are being heard.

FINDS COMPROMISE. Good decisions often come from working through different perspectives and finding a practical path forward.

IS DECISIVE. Once the discussion is complete, leadership also means being prepared to make a decision and move ahead.

I have served on Grand Valley Council for more than five years. I have been involved with AMO and ROMA and have experience representing municipal interests at the provincial level.

Professionally, I am a sales executive and a current business owner. My wife and I understand what it means to operate a business, manage expenses, build relationships and make difficult financial decisions. I believe those experiences can bring a practical and balanced perspective to the mayor's chair.

Growing Without Losing Who We Are

This election is about the future of Grand Valley and the kind of leadership we need to make thoughtful decisions about where our community goes next.

I grew up in Hillsburgh, and I understand what people mean when they talk about protecting a small-town feeling. It is knowing your neighbours. It is being able to say hello to people on your street. It is walking downtown and recognizing the people you meet.

Those things matter.

I believe Grand Valley can welcome new families, attract new businesses and prepare for the future while still protecting the character that makes this community special. We can be financially responsible. We can plan for growth. We can support our business community. And we can still remain a place where people feel connected to their neighbours and proud to call Grand Valley home.
